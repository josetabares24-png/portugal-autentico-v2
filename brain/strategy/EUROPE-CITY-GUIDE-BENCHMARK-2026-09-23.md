# European city-guide benchmark — 2026-09-23

## Question

What existing city-guide/media models in Lisbon and Europe are actually relevant to the product Estaba en Lisboa is trying to become?

This is not a visual moodboard.

The benchmark compares:
- editorial model;
- information architecture;
- trust;
- recurring usefulness;
- owned audience;
- monetization;
- maintenance burden;
- patterns to avoid.

## DIRECT LISBON COMPETITORS / REFERENCES

### Lisboa Secreta
Source:
https://lisboasecreta.co/sobre-nos/

Model:
- local city media;
- news + events + food + culture + "things to do";
- social-first distribution;
- branded content;
- ticketing through Fever.

Strength:
- strong reason to return frequently;
- covers what changed in the city, not only evergreen tourism;
- highly shareable topics;
- commercial model extends beyond affiliate links.

Risk / what NOT to copy:
- network-template feel;
- heavy use of "secret", "best", "unmissable", "authentic";
- event/content volume requires a newsroom;
- recency treadmill can create a lot of low-value output.

Lesson for Estaba en Lisboa:
Use the **current-change layer**, not the content-volume model.

A small site can be useful by explaining closures, transport changes, ticket rules, price changes and important openings that materially affect a visitor.

### GoLisbon
Sources:
https://www.golisbon.com/
https://www.golisbon.com/about-us.html

Model:
- comprehensive old-school city guide;
- deep destination taxonomy;
- neighbourhoods, monuments, streets, transport, accommodation.

Strength:
- breadth;
- stable evergreen architecture;
- clear mental model of the city.

Risk:
- "complete guide" mentality can become a catalogue;
- completeness itself is not differentiation;
- large taxonomy is expensive to keep fresh.

Lesson:
Depth matters, but Estaba en Lisboa should not try to win by having a page for everything.

### LisbonLisboaPortugal
Sources:
https://lisbonlisboaportugal.com/index.html
https://lisbonlisboaportugal.com/lisbon-tour/about-us.html

Model:
- independent Lisbon guide;
- creator-led authority;
- large evergreen library;
- affiliate-funded;
- practical details checked against official sources;
- explicit author/context.

Strength:
- trust;
- clear authorship;
- factual maintenance;
- direct visitor questions;
- affiliate monetization attached to practical content.

Important observed claim:
The site says it has 189 Lisbon guides and that practical details such as prices, opening hours, bus routes and time-slot policies are checked against official sources and in person.

Lesson:
This is the strongest direct model for our **factual discipline**, not necessarily for our voice or design.

### Portugalist
Source:
https://www.portugalist.com/about/

Model:
- independent information product about moving/living in Portugal;
- deep practical guides;
- comparisons;
- book product;
- strong "clear answer" positioning.

Strength:
- turns confusing questions into decision pages;
- clear author identity;
- product extends into an owned book.

Lesson:
The valuable pattern is not relocation content. It is **decision-grade information** and building a durable product from the knowledge base.

## EUROPEAN PRODUCT REFERENCES

### Romewise — Rome
Sources:
https://www.romewise.com/about-romewise.html
https://romewise.com/

Model:
- creator-led city guide;
- deep first-time visitor information;
- heavy practical orientation;
- clear personal expertise.

Strength:
- answers the long tail of real visitor questions;
- author credibility is explicit;
- the site feels like accumulated knowledge, not generated inventory.

Risk:
- very personal voice can become hard to scale;
- we must not manufacture lived experience to imitate it.

Lesson:
Use real authorship where true, and research where not personally experienced.

### The Earful Tower — Paris
Sources:
https://theearfultower.com/
https://theearfultower.com/about/
https://theearfultower.com/product/guide-book/

Model:
- city media brand around one recognisable creator;
- podcast + video + blog + newsletter;
- membership;
- walking tours;
- books;
- paid PDF guide.

Observed 2026 product:
- paid Paris PDF guide: 29.99 €;
- member/subscriber ecosystem;
- direct products beyond ads/affiliate.

Strength:
- turns audience trust into multiple products;
- website is one part of a media/product system.

Lesson:
This is a strong **future business model** for Estaba en Lisboa, but only after we prove audience and newsletter growth.

Do not create a paid guide just because this model exists.

### Londonist — London
Source:
https://londonist.com/about_us

Model:
- digital city publication;
- news, reviews, events, history, future, quirks.

Strength:
- recurring usefulness;
- treats the city as something living, not a static tourist destination.

Lesson:
A Lisbon publication can include selected city changes/history/current utility without becoming a daily-news operation.

### This is Athens
Sources:
https://www.thisisathens.org/
https://www.thisisathens.org/neighbourhoods

Model:
- official guide, but strongly editorial;
- neighbourhoods as a central exploration system;
- editor picks;
- events;
- local walks.

Strength:
- architecture follows how visitors understand a city;
- neighbourhood pages connect articles, places and activities.

Lesson:
Our existing Barrios cluster can eventually become a real exploration layer, but only after the current content map and internal-click data justify a public hub.

### Visit Copenhagen
Sources:
https://www.visitcopenhagen.com/
https://www.visitcopenhagen.com/copenhagen/areas/neighborhoods/the-copenhagen-neighbourhood-guide

Model:
- official tourism publication;
- neighbourhoods;
- day trips;
- food;
- current events;
- local itineraries;
- podcast/insider content.

Strength:
- mixes planning and editorial inspiration without forcing everything into a store.

Lesson:
Itinerary content is useful when it is one editorial format among many, not the identity of the whole site.

### Spotted by Locals
Sources:
https://www.spottedbylocals.com/lisbon/
https://www.spottedbylocals.com/about-us/

Model:
- paid city-guide access;
- local contributors;
- 85+ cities;
- mobile guide;
- Lisbon access currently advertised at 9.99 €.

Strength:
- clear paid value proposition;
- named human contributors;
- recurring updates;
- portable/on-trip utility.

Risk:
- its language leans heavily on "hidden gems", "authentic", "real locals" and anti-tourist framing;
- those phrases are already saturated and conflict with Mente Lisboa's voice.

Lesson:
The paid-guide/app model is interesting; the "hidden local secrets" positioning is not.

## CROSS-MARKET PATTERNS

Across the strongest references, the useful recurring patterns are:

1. **Named editorial authority**
   - a reader understands who is responsible for the information.

2. **Neighbourhoods as a city mental model**
   - not just categories in a CMS.

3. **Freshness as product**
   - what changed, what closed, how prices/access work now.

4. **Owned audience**
   - newsletter/member relationship is treated as an asset.

5. **A product beyond pageviews**
   - paid guide, membership, tour, book, direct partnership or other owned offer.

6. **Evergreen + current**
   - good city media is not only "10 things to see" and not only daily news.

7. **Editorial selection**
   - the site says what a page is for and what to do next.

## WHERE ESTABA EN LISBOA SHOULD NOT COMPETE

Do not compete on:
- most pages;
- most events;
- "hidden gems";
- claiming to be more local than everyone else;
- generic "authentic Lisbon";
- a huge ticket marketplace;
- social-media clickbait volume.

Those positions are already occupied by organisations with larger teams, networks or stronger historical authority.

## OPPORTUNITY 1 — CURRENT VISITOR-IMPACT UPDATES

Internal working concept:
**visitor-impact updates**, not a public brand name yet.

Problem:
Evergreen Lisbon guides become wrong when:
- transport changes;
- a lift closes;
- prices change;
- ticket rules change;
- a monument reopens;
- a new reservation system appears.

Why this exists:
The Santa Justa audit proved that stale operational information can materially damage usefulness.

Differential:
Not "news about Lisbon".
Only changes that affect a visitor's decision.

Possible examples:
- Santa Justa closure/reopening;
- Carris route changes affecting major tourist corridors;
- monument ticket changes;
- airport access changes;
- new reservation rules.

Distribution:
- update the canonical article/activity first;
- optionally surface a small change log later;
- newsletter only when the change is important.

SEO:
Primarily freshness/support for existing URLs, not a volume-content play.

Business:
Trust improves downstream affiliate/service conversion.

Metric:
- organic retention on affected URLs;
- CTR/position after factual update;
- internal continuation;
- newsletter signups from useful updates.

First experiment:
Do not create a new section yet.
Track the next 5 material factual changes and update their canonical pages. Measure whether this maintenance improves or protects search/user behaviour.

## OPPORTUNITY 2 — NEIGHBOURHOODS AS EDITORIAL EXPLORATION

Problem:
Visitors think in areas during the trip, while websites often expose CMS categories.

Existing asset:
Estaba en Lisboa already has:
- barrios-imprescindibles
- Alfama
- Graça
- Baixa
- Belém
- Chiado/Bairro Alto
- Mouraria
- Parque das Nações

Differential:
A neighbourhood page should answer:
- why go;
- what is actually there;
- what changed;
- how much time;
- what connects naturally;
- what is not worth crossing the city for.

Do not build:
a grid of generic cards called "Explore neighbourhoods".

First experiment:
Wait for current `select_content` data.
If Barrios pages show meaningful continuation, prototype one neighbourhood landing experience for review **without merging visual changes before approval**.

Metric:
- internal click-through;
- pages/session from neighbourhood entry;
- organic queries by neighbourhood;
- downstream commercial click when relevant.

## OPPORTUNITY 3 — OWNED LISBON PRODUCT

Future model references:
The Earful Tower / Spotted by Locals / Portugalist.

Possible future formats:
- paid digital Lisbon guide;
- compact neighbourhood guide;
- updated offline trip pack;
- member/subscriber edition;
- book later.

Do NOT build now.

Gate:
Only revisit after:
- newsletter signup is measurable;
- audience shows repeat/owned demand;
- we identify repeated questions worth packaging;
- the product contains material unavailable as a simple bundle of free pages.

Business value:
Owned revenue with less dependence on affiliate commissions.

## OPPORTUNITY 4 — AUTHORSHIP + VERIFICATION AS A TRUST SYSTEM

This is the most immediate reusable pattern from LisbonLisboaPortugal, Romewise and Portugalist.

For dynamic practical pages, we should progressively make it easy to know:
- who wrote/reviewed it;
- when it was checked;
- what official source supports changing facts.

We already began this operationally in Activity freshness.

Do not add a large visual trust box across the site without approval.

First step:
continue storing `lastVerified` and official source data in structured content where the fact is dynamic.

## PRIORITY DECISION

The competitor benchmark does **not** create a new P0.

Current P0 remains measurement of the real internal journey and current SEO experiments.

The benchmark changes our future direction:

**Estaba en Lisboa should become a small, current, decision-useful Lisbon publication with a recognisable human author and eventually an owned product — not a bigger catalogue.**

## WHAT TO STEAL / WHAT NOT TO STEAL

Steal:
- Romewise / LisbonLisboaPortugal: responsibility and depth.
- Earful Tower: audience → owned product.
- This is Athens / Copenhagen: neighbourhood mental model.
- Londonist / Lisboa Secreta: city changes create reasons to return.
- Spotted by Locals: people will pay for genuinely useful portable city knowledge.

Do not steal:
- hidden-gem clichés;
- "live like a local";
- secret-city branding;
- mass listicles;
- generic card grids;
- newsroom volume;
- network-template language;
- artificial first-person experience.
